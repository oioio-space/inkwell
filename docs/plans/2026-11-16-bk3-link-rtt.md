# BK3 — the direct link RTT window is derived from the probe's sampling law

Rigour: light (test calibration; no product change). Symptom: `TestLinkRTTFromProbe/direct/seed2` failed once in a
local CI run with « RTT = 62.808314ms, want within [62.950318ms, 83.933757ms] (true median 69.944798ms) ».

**Root cause: the window, not the probe.** The test runs in a synctest bubble (controlled time): load cannot change a
round trip there, and a reading below R is not a load effect. The figure it checks is the lower median of the window's
samples (`protocol/linkprobe` `Prober.RTT`, spec Annex A.1): one PROBE a second over 30 s, 31 samples on this lossless
path, each one round trip of two independent one-way draws `3 ms + 20 ms + U[0, 24 ms)`, so a triangular law on
[46, 94) ms with median R = 70 ms. The lower median of 31 draws is below x exactly when at least 16 draws are, a
binomial in the path's CDF. For the old window's floor 0.9 R (P(draw < 0.9 R) = 0.249) that is **1.35e-3 per seed**,
2.7 % of the runs of 20 seeds: the failure was the window's expected false-alarm rate. The window [0.9 R, 1.2 R] was
chosen by eye from a few runs (gates/C5S/SIMPLIFY.md), never from this law; it was asymmetric too (the ceiling's
false-alarm rate is ~1e-9).

**Measured, the probe is unbiased.** 800 seeds (`-count=40` of the 20-seed subtest, figure logged per seed) against
100 000 simulated lower medians of 31 path draws: mean 69.90 ms vs 69.99, standard deviation 2.37 vs 2.27, 1st and
99th percentiles 64.4 / 75.5 vs 64.6 / 75.4; one seed of 800 fell under the old floor (62.79 ms), where the law
predicts 1.1. No low tail beyond the law, so no late send timestamp, PTO truncation or clock mismatch is at work:
the product's figure (Annex A.1, the level's lower median) is what it claims, and the defect is the test's.

**Fix (test only).** `rttWindows` derives both windows from the path's distribution (`pathRTTs`, the same draws R
comes from) at a false-alarm rate `rttAlpha` = 1e-7 per side: per seed, [57.7, 82.3] ms (0.825 R to 1.177 R); for the
lower median of the 20 seeds' figures, a binomial of the per-seed law, [66.5, 73.1] ms (0.951 R to 1.045 R). The
per-seed check alone would have lost the anti-vacuity (quic-go's SmoothedRTT reads about 0.8–0.93 R), so the new
check over the seeds carries it: an estimator biased like SmoothedRTT (median over the seeds below 0.95 R, asserted
in the same test) falls outside. A run fails falsely with probability below 5e-6. `TestRTTWindowCalibration` (pure,
instant) pins the derivation: the old floor's 1.35e-3, each derived window at most `rttAlpha`, and the floor over the
seeds above 0.95 R.

**Alternatives set aside.** Widening the per-seed floor alone (loses the bias check); more probe samples or a longer
run (changes the product's window or doubles the test's cost to hide the same tail); fewer seeds (same tail per seed).

**Sources.** The law of a sample quantile is binomial in the parent CDF, P(X(k) ≤ x) = P(Bin(n, F(x)) ≥ k), the
basis of distribution-free quantile bounds: H. A. David and H. N. Nagaraja, *Order Statistics*, 3rd ed., Wiley 2003,
§2.1 and §7.1; W. J. Conover, *Practical Nonparametric Statistics*, 3rd ed., Wiley 1999, §3.2. It applies here
because the probe's figure is exactly an order statistic (rank ⌊(n−1)/2⌋) of independent draws of the simulated path.
