# Method v2 (2026-11-12) — agreed by a debate between two model families

Why: since 2026-11-10 about 97 % of the lines written were tooling and documents; the last user-visible product commit
was SP1b on 2026-11-09; C5S's gate spent 0.6 of 302 minutes on the block's own product tests and failed six levels down
on an inherited micro-benchmark guard. Two independent studies (Claude Opus, Codex), then three debate rounds, ended in
full agreement on the rules below. Sources: SQLite testing, Chromium CQ, Go LUCI and performance monitoring, Rust bors,
TigerBeetle VOPR, FoundationDB simulation, AWS on TLA+, DORA/Accelerate, Google's Software Engineering book (links in
the studies, `docs/reviews/codex-coverage.md`).

## Rules (each replaces a practice; each has a metric)

1. **WIP:** one block implementing, one candidate verifying; one worktree per active block, about three checkouts in
   all. Metric: blocks open at once, oldest open block.
2. **One heavy campaign at a time** on the laptop; no heavy agent work during it; long runs detached and watched;
   liveness checked without the rtk filter. Metric: duplicate or orphan runs, invalidations caused by orchestration.
3. **Review before freeze, then after the smoke.** The spec and draft gate get a second-family review before the freeze
   (a review there is expected to find); the code gets one review after the smoke (it should find little; a major finding
   there triggers a design decision, not a patch). At most two rounds per block. The post-smoke review contains the
   simplification section (`gates/<bloc>/SIMPLIFY.md`) and checks that every new technical decision in the diff is
   recorded with sources in `docs/plans/` or `docs/adr/`. Metric: major findings after a campaign started.
4. **Rigour by risk.** "Complete" (models with mutants, statistical acceptance) is for a closed list: peer wire and
   state, trust and admission, the sandbox boundary, bytes from a peer, and three or more concurrent actors on shared
   durable state (e.g. OI). Anything else is standard (named tests, decision tables, a gate of at most 45 min, a plan of
   at most two pages) or light (tooling and docs: tests and one review, a one-page plan). An exception names the invariant
   and a concrete failure and is reviewed before freeze. Caps trigger re-splitting, never the removal of an indispensable
   proof. "When in doubt, the level above" is replaced by "standard plus one plan review". Metric: plan size and gate
   minutes per tier.
5. **Iteration loop:** inner loop in minutes, a 20–30 min smoke, then the frozen campaign once on the final candidate;
   `mise run gate -- --resume <lot>` after an incident. Metric: full campaigns per block.
6. **Tiers of execution, same obligations.** T0 pre-commit (≤ 1 min), T1 pre-merge (≤ 45 min: tests, smoke), T2 the
   block's frozen campaign (statistical repeats, e2e mutants, native budgets of affected paths), T3 the release
   checklist (clean install, first launch, two-person journey, recovery, the R1 acceptance list). **Everything a block's
   own frozen gate names passes before `main`, on the exact candidate**; post-merge runs only add evidence. New gates
   name only the maximal gates of their regression closure, re-run an ancestor campaign only if the block can affect its
   inputs, and never call a gate that another named gate already chains.
7. **`next` → `main`.** A candidate green on T1 is committed to a local `next` branch; the queue fast-forwards `main` to
   the exact commit whose frozen campaign passed (`main` is its ancestor; later commits on `next` never ride along;
   `main` is never rewritten). A red campaign is fixed or reverted on `next`, and the result is a new candidate. Work
   started from `next` is provisional: when its predecessor changes it is rebased and its T1 runs again. Nothing is
   added to a candidate while its campaign runs.
8. **Performance.** User-facing budgets (start, keystroke) of the paths a block affects are measured once per candidate
   before acceptance; the integrated R1 candidate keeps its native qualification. A micro-benchmark guard blocks only if
   its package is in the block's inputs and the slowdown reproduces in a paired, interleaved run (PB's machinery);
   otherwise it is advisory trend data. A frozen obligation is replaced only by a reviewed guarantee change.
9. **"Never weaken" protects the contract.** Moving a criterion between tiers, excluding samples, or widening a budget is
   a contract change: stated, compared old/new on held-out data, with a negative control showing a real regression still
   fails, recorded in `gates/guard-overrides.json`.
10. **Statistics.** The frozen 30 repeats stay: they detect regressions (zero failures in 30 bounds the rate at about
    9.5 %, 95 % one-sided); reliability claims come from simulation with many seeds (synctest, natNet), real netsim checks
    the adapter's assumptions.
11. **Mutation.** Frozen thresholds bind. New gates keep 95 % where the population is relevant; an e2e mutant moves to a
    faster level only after both kill the same fault for the intended reason (a timeout or build failure is no kill),
    recorded; every critical survivor gets an explicit disposition.
12. **Tooling on demonstrated return.** A tooling block only fixes a measured false verdict or saves at least two hours
    a week, fits in a day, is light or standard, and starts only when no product block is implemented-but-unmerged;
    tooling at most 20 % of a week's commits. Metric: tooling share of commits.
13. **Push** each accepted block; `main` always green. Pre-push runs formal and netsim only when their complete inputs
    changed (models, adapters, configuration, harnesses), and runs them when the impact is uncertain; GitHub CI runs the
    full set. Metric: hours with a red `main`, age of the last push.
14. **Documents.** One current status and priority list (`PROGRESS.md`); `docs/exigences.md` stable; ADRs only for
    architecture; `docs/autonomous-decisions.md` only for durable decisions.
15. **Weekly look** at delivery: lead time per block (start → `main`), accepted blocks per day, share of time in
    verification, review rounds, escaped regressions (DORA adapted to a single integrator).

## Removed or changed

- Reminders injected on every prompt or edit (`research-grounding.sh`, `modern-go-context.sh`, `benchmark-context.sh`)
  and the start-of-session `openrouter-check.sh`: removed. Research is required where decisions are written (rule 3);
  modern Go stays enforced by the `modernize` linter and the `use-modern-go` skill; the benchstat rule for HOT_PATHS
  stays in CLAUDE.md.
- `docs/objectif-en-cours.md` is folded into `PROGRESS.md` (one list).
- Stopped: GC, MV, the general RG programme (only the blocking L0 oracle is fixed, rule 9), GP beyond its cheap half
  (shared caches, criteria writing only to their evidence directory), HP's P/E placement (its slot-exclusion half stays
  a candidate only if rule 12 holds).
- Kept as essential: frozen gate before code and the anti-weakening guard, named-test checks, red-first fixes,
  heavy.sh memory caps, executable specs/models/mutants for the protocol and trust, second-family review (earlier), only
  Claude commits after reading the diff, `netsim/affected.py`.
- To measure before changing: the cost of `post-edit-check.sh` per edit (batch it only if its p95 exceeds a few
  seconds).
