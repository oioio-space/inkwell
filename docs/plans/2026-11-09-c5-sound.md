# C5S: a sound clause C5 (the level shown follows the injected loss)

Date: 2026-11-09. Block C5S, step 1 of the definition of done (« specify first, executably »).
Requirements: W32 (impaired-network tests), W33 (see the link quality). Executable specification:
`scripts/netsim/c5model.py`, tests `scripts/netsim/c5model_test.py`. Method:
`docs/plans/2026-11-08-spec-executable.md`.

**Problem.** Clause C5 of netsim scenario 5 (NI-1, `frontend/e2e/netsim.spec.ts`, the clause at line 573) fails at
random, with and without SI1 (`git show si1b:gates/SI1b/SIMPLIFY.md`, « C5 A/B »): P1 at 0.5 % under its
1 % floor, P3 at 0.7 % with no injected loss. The interval [1 %, 10 %] / ≤ 0.5 % was chosen by hand.

**Outcome.** The number is not what W33 says it is (section 1); the decision of what it must mean
(section 2); a statistical model of netem, the traffic and quic-go's loss detection (section 3).
**Revised on 2026-11-10 (user decision after review 3 of the probe, ADR 0036 « Levels »):** the person sees a
level (good, fair, poor, unknown), not a permille figure, and C5 becomes a classification test whose rule has a
false-failure rate ≤ 1e-3 per run and a power ≥ 0.999 against five mutants (section 4); the changes to make once
the gate is frozen (section 5); the proposed gate (section 6). Sections 1 to 3 keep the history of the figure.

## 1. What the reported loss is today

### The chain, end to end

| Step | Where | What happens |

So the figure is, per endpoint and for its **outgoing** direction only:

> (ack-eliciting packets this endpoint **declared** lost, spurious declarations and ECN-CE marks
> included) / (**all** packets it sent, ACK-only ones included), over the per-second steps of the last
> ≈ 30 s (29-31 s, plus up to 2 s of sampling and push staleness), at 0.1 % resolution.

### How many packets it rests on

The C6 counters of the passing SP1b runs (`gates/SP1b/netsim-regression.log`, seeds 1-3) show 857 to 917
packets per qdisc over P1, which lasts about 45 s for the counters (the profile is set before pairing;
the whole test took 2.1 min): about 19-20 packets/s per hop and direction, **≈ 580 packets per 30 s
window**. quic-go acknowledges every second ack-eliciting packet (`received_packet_tracker.go:82`,
`packetsBeforeAck = 2`) or after 25 ms, so in two-way typing about one packet in three is ACK-only: ≈ 390
ack-eliciting packets, of which ≈ 390 × 3.57 % ≈ **14 are lost** in a window. Loss in netem's
Gilbert-Elliott chain comes in bursts (mean 3.3 packets in the bad state), which triples the variance of
a count (dispersion `1 + 2 Var(ℓ) λ/(1-λ) / L(1-L)` ≈ 3.1 with λ = 1 - p - r = 0.69): the numerator's
relative spread is ≈ 45 %. The figure also reads the injected loss times the ack-eliciting share (2/3):
≈ 2.4 %. The 12 observed P1/P2 values of SP1b average **2.38 %** (range 1.0-4.6 %).

### What explains the observed failures

- **P1 at 0.5 %** (and every failure of the floor): ≈ 14 clustered events, read at 2/3 of their value,
  against a fixed floor of 1 %. The model reproduces it: the old rule fails **24.6 %** of 1000 simulated
  runs of today's code at today's traffic (`Findings.test_old_rule_is_flaky_on_the_code_of_today`;
  observed: 1 failure in 4 runs of the A side of the SI1b A/B).
- **P3 at 0.7 %**: not a stale window. At 40 s into P3 the window covers sends and declarations after
  ≈ P3 + 9 s, and P2's last losses are declared within about one PTO (≈ 0.4 s) of being sent; the
  packets the qdisc replacement flushes at the phase change (`impair`: `del` then `add`) are declared at
  P3 + 0.4 s at the latest. It is **spurious declarations**: wifi-clean keeps a 5 ms normal jitter per
  hop, netem's tfifo sorts by send time, so packets overtake each other, and with quic-go's initial
  time threshold (9/8 RTT) the model counts ≈ 2.3 spurious declarations per 30 s window at today's
  traffic (P(≥ 3, i.e. ≥ 0.5 %) ≈ 0.37), ≈ 1.1 after one adaptation (10/8) and ≈ 0 from 12/8 on
  (`Findings.test_jitter_alone_makes_quic_declare_losses`). quic-go adapts only after it has seen
  spurious losses, which is why P3 reads 0 in most runs and 0.5-0.7 % in some.
- **The four netem chains are one.** `topology.sh` gives every qdisc the same `seed $seed`, and each
  packet makes the same draws: nat-a/eth0 and nat-b/eth0 dropped exactly 21/21, 12/12 and 13/13 packets in
  SP1b seeds 1-3. Each phase reseeds with the same value, so P1 and P2 replay the same loss sequence by
  packet index. The model assumes independent chains; section 5 makes that true.

## 2. What the metric must mean (decision)

W33 shows a person the quality of a link. A declaration is the sender's guess (RFC 9002 §6.1: a packet
is declared lost when a later one is acknowledged and it is kPacketThreshold = 3 packets or kTimeThreshold
= 9/8 RTT older; « Implementations can detect spurious retransmissions and increase the packet or time
reordering threshold », §6.1, §6.1.1-6.1.2). An ACK-only packet is not ack-eliciting (§2) and its loss is
never detected at all. An ECN-CE mark is not a loss. None of these is what the network did.

**Decision: the loss figure is the share of the packets this endpoint sent to the peer on the direct
connection, over the last 30 s, that the peer did not receive intact.** Reordering, duplication, a
packet resent too early or a congestion mark do not count; corruption and drops do. The scope stays
`path` for a direct link; a relayed link keeps `firstHop` (out of C5).

### Can quic-go give it?

| Version | Spurious declarations | Denominator |
|---|---|---|
| v0.64.1 (pinned, `go.mod:15`, required by go-libp2p v0.51.0) | detected, **not subtracted**; ECN-CE counted as losses | all packets: diluted by the ACK-only share |
| v0.65.0 (2026-11-03) | same | same |
| master since d5a8ae6 (PR #6169, merged 2026-11-08, fixes #6167 and #6168; unreleased) | subtracted while tracked (3 PTO, 64 entries) | same |

No version counts the ack-eliciting packets sent, the only denominator `PacketsLost` can be divided by.
go-libp2p v0.51.0 sets `quic.Config.Tracer` itself (`quicreuse/connmgr.go:115`) and offers no option to
replace it, so an in-process qlog recorder (`qlog.PacketSent`, `qlog.SpuriousLoss`) is not reachable.

### Two honest designs

- **D1, sender side:** `(PacketsLost with #6169) / (ack-eliciting packets sent)`. Needs a quic-go release
  with #6169 and a new upstream counter. It estimates the loss from the ack-eliciting packets only, a
  sample of ⅔ of them (½ in the model's worst case): its residual against the network truth is
  **1.85 times wider** (σ_z 1.14 against 0.62, measured in 2026-11-09 with the former `calibrate` command; `test_d1_needs_several_times_the_traffic_of_d2` keeps the ratio), so the same rule needs ≈ 3.4 times the
  traffic.
- **D2, receiver count (chosen):** the RTCP receiver report of RFC 3550 §6.4.1 (« fraction lost » =
  expected minus received, counted by the receiver) and WebRTC's `remote-inbound-rtp`, over QUIC. Once a
  second the sender writes a PROBE (a sequence number) and notes its `PacketsSent`; the receiver, when it
  reads the probe, answers ECHO (the sequence number and its `PacketsReceived` for the connection). An
  echo whose round trip exceeds `srtt + 4·rttvar + max_ack_delay` came from a resent probe or echo and is
  discarded (its sample would be late). Over the newest accepted sample and the oldest one at most 30 s
  older: `loss = 1 − ΔReceived / ΔSent`, clamped to [0, 1]. In v0.64.1 `PacketsReceived` counts packets
  decrypted and processed, after the duplicate check (`connection.go:1229`, `:1745`, `:1765`), so a
  duplicate or a reordered packet is not a loss and a corrupted one is. It needs no quic-go change, is
  immune to spurious declarations, ECN-CE and ACK-only dilution, and measures exactly what netem's
  counters measure. Its cost: a new peer-to-peer message (spec, ADR, vectors, fuzzed decoder), about 2
  small messages per second per link, and a peer that can lie about its own link (display only,
  clamped, ignored if `received` decreases).

D2 is chosen. If the protocol change is refused, D1 is the fallback, with the quic-go upgrade and an
upstream counter as prerequisites and ≈ 200 packets/s of background traffic in the test.

## 3. The executable model (`scripts/netsim/c5model.py`)

Standard library only; every number of this plan comes from it.

1. **netem** (`netem_hop`), from Linux 7.2 `net/sched/sch_netem.c` (`netem_enqueue`, `loss_gilb_ell`) and
   iproute2 `tc/q_netem.c`: per packet, the duplication draw; the Gilbert-Elliott transition draw, then
   the loss draw **of the state the packet found** (tc stores `1-h` as `h`, the kernel loses in the bad
   state when `rand > h`: `gemodel 1% 30% 50% 0.1%` is p = 0.01, r = 0.30, 50 % loss in bad, 0.1 % in
   good, per-hop loss 1.71 %); a duplicated packet that loses its draw still leaves once; the duplicate
   is re-enqueued at the root (`tc_depth`, kernel 9552b11e3eda) and draws its own fate; corruption of
   survivors; delay `delay + jitter·N(0,1)` bounded to ±4σ and clamped at 0, sorted by send time (so jitter
   reorders); `reorder P%` (tc sets gap 1) sends P % at once. Two hops per direction (sender's NAT egress
   to the WAN, receiver's NAT egress to its LAN), a share `foreign` of other flows on each qdisc, the
   qdisc counters over the test's window. Path loss per direction 3.57 % in P1 and P2.
2. **Traffic and windows** (`loss_window`): our packets as a Poisson stream; the product's window ends
   0-2 s before the test reads it (sampler step and push) and spans 30 ± 1 s; the qdisc window is the
   30 s before the read. D2's boundaries are probes, a lost or late probe moving the boundary 1 s; the crossings
   of each PROBE are modelled with no priority (review 3): the packets sent while it waits in the sender, the
   ±1 of the responder's count, a read up to 20 ms late, a lost PROBE resent after 9/8 RTT, head-of-line
   blocking behind a lost one. `trace` runs a whole scenario second by second (the five phases back to back,
   the classifier at every sampler step, a read every second with the qdisc counters).
3. **quic-go's loss detection** (`QuicLossDetector`): RFC 9002 §5.3 RTT estimator and §6.1 detection as
   v0.64.1 implements them (time threshold `(8 + e)/8 · max(srtt, latest_rtt)`, packet threshold 3
   disabled after a spurious loss, `e` doubled up to 8 when a spurious loss was reordered by more than the
   current threshold, 64-entry tracker pruned after 3 PTO) and the receiver's ACK policy
   (`received_packet_tracker.go`: every 2 ack-eliciting packets, at once for a reordered or gap-opening
   packet, else after 25 ms). In P4 it starts fully adapted (e = 8, packet threshold off): the fewest
   spurious losses, the worst case for the power.
4. **Estimators**: honest (D2), D1, the code of today (`incumbent`); the levels shown by the honest prober
   and by the mutants of section 4 (always good, always poor, swapped, no hysteresis, the old figure).
   (Before 2026-11-10 the mutants were figures: zero, halved, doubled, stale, cumulative, spurious.)
5. **Validation against what was observed**: today's code at today's traffic reads 0.67 × the injected
   loss (observed mean 2.38 % for 3.57 % × 0.67 = 2.39 %), fails the old rule in 24.6 % of runs, and reads
   ≥ 0.5 % in P3 with an unadapted threshold in 37 % of windows (section 1).

**Two formalisms, each for what it proves.** C5 is a probabilistic claim (a false-failure rate and a
power); TLC explores every interleaving but carries no probabilities, so the clause rests on this
statistical model. A probabilistic model checker (PRISM, Storm) could compute the Gilbert-Elliott count
distribution exactly, but it is not in the pinned toolchain and the windows hold thousands of packets;
Monte Carlo with exact Clopper-Pearson bounds gives the two bounds C5 needs. The probe's own state
machines (late echoes, the outstanding bound, the window, restarts, forged counts, a silent peer) are
interleaving questions: they are checked by TLA+ `LinkProbe.tla` (ADR 0036) and by the corpus scripts.

## 4. The acceptance rule: a classification

The product shows a level per link (spec §8.5.5 step 6, Annex A.1 `quality`): loss boundaries 1 % and 10 %
(bands 0.5-2.5 % and 5-15 %), round-trip boundaries 500 ms and 2 s; the thresholds, their sources and the
derivation of the bands are in ADR 0036 « Levels ». C5 checks that the level shown follows the loss netem
inflicts.

**Phases** (40 s each, P5 120 s): P0 `wifi-entry` (a proposed profile: independent loss of 1.75 % on the
path, between the good | fair midpoint 1 % and its rise threshold 2.5 %, so the level entered from « unknown » is
judged; the test types twice as fast during P0), then P1 `wifi`, P2 `wifi-far`, P3 `wifi-clean`, P4
`wifi-reorder`, P5 `wifi-edge` (path loss 0.99 %, on the boundary). Traffic: 120 packets/s per direction (240
during P0). **What the test reads**: every sampler step the prober logs for each direction (its `quality` and
`lossPermille`; the netsim build logs each step, so no coalesced Links push hides one), and the cumulative qstats
counters of the direction's two hops in path order (AB: nat-a/eth0 then nat-b/eth1; BA: nat-b/eth0 then
nat-a/eth1) every second. For a step at t ≥ 33 s, the truth `T*` of every window its figure may cover (ends 0 to
3 s before, spans 28 to 30 s) is `1 − (1 − d₁)(1 − d₂)(1 − c)²`, `d_i` the share dropped on hop i and c the
profile's corruption; `[T*_lo, T*_hi]` is their range and `n` the fewest first-hop packets.

| # | Rule (constants in `c5model.RULE`) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | every step: the level shown is **exactly** the level of spec §8.5.5 step 6 applied to the figures shown so far, the round-trip level good (every profile of scenario 5 is below 400 ms) | the level follows its figure and its hysteresis: a level that never falls back, rises at the wrong place or enters from « unknown » at the wrong threshold is caught at the first figure where it differs (review 4) |
| R2 | every step from 33 s: `n ≥ 3000` and the figure within `[T*_lo − tol, T*_hi + tol]`, `tol = 1.5·√(T*_hi/n) + c/n` | the figure follows the truth: a figure that is stale, cumulative or diluted is caught |
| R3 | P0, P1, P2: mean `T* ≥ 1 %` over their steps | the impairment happened (anti-vacuity) |

`c` is the crossing term of the figure, in packets (ADR 0036 « Levels »): 22 on P0, P1 and P5, 32 on P2, 24 on P3, 52 on P4, from the largest needed
over 4000 traces with a margin. `1.5·√(T*/n)` covers the foreign packets of the qdiscs (10 % in the model). The rule
is applied by `c5model.py verdict` (one implementation, used by the test and checked by the model tests).

**Results** (seeded, deterministic; `c5model.py report`, 3000 runs, both directions):

| Honest runs rejected | False failures (95 % upper) | Each required mutant rejected | Power (95 % lower) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 / 3000 | ≤ 1.0e-3 | 3000 / 3000 | ≥ 0.999 |

Required mutants: always good, always poor, always fair, swapped, no hysteresis, sticky (a level that never falls
back), the entry from « unknown » against `up`, the loss thresholds ×2, the round-trip thresholds ÷10 (all R1), the
old figure, M5b and a 60 s window (R2). Halved thresholds: 2997 / 3000 (reported). The reviewer's checks of
review 4, adapted to this rule (`rule_check`, `m5b_check`, `w2_check`), reject every one of their mutants.

## 7. Open questions

1. D2 is a protocol addition (ADR 0036). If it is refused, D1 needs a quic-go release with #6169, an
   upstream counter of ack-eliciting packets sent, and ≈ 3.4 times the traffic in the test.

   turns good into fair, even fully adapted); checked on the real stack only by mutant M5h.
7. The level's thresholds rest on sources for calls and broadband (ADR 0036 « Levels »), not on a study of
   collaborative editors; the first user sessions of W33 should confirm that fair and poor match what people
   feel.

   point; R0 makes too little traffic red rather than green.

   (installed: 6.17): T* uses the nominal 0.1 % per hop; with a newer `tc` the measured count replaces it.
5. The CI runner's kernel may predate the netem duplicate fix of 2026-05 (9552b11e3eda); the effect on the
   counters is below 1e-4.
