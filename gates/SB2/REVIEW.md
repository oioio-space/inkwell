# SB2 - reviews of the specification

Specification: `docs/adr/0044-sandbox-tiers.md`, `docs/plans/2026-11-14-sb2-sandbox-tiers.md`, draft `gates/SB2a.toml`.

## Round 1 — Codex on revision 1 (REJECT), 2026-11-14

Two critical, twelve major, one minor. Dispositions by the integrator. « To measure first » means the finding, or our
own claim it corrects, rests on a fact nobody has measured: the probe `scripts/sandbox-probe.sh`
(`.github/workflows/sandbox-probe.yml`, started by hand on stock Ubuntu 24.04 runners, amd64 and arm64) answers it
before revision 2 is written. Revision 2 is not written yet.

| Id | Severity | Finding (evidence) | Disposition | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | critical | The ADR's tier table has « Landlock × cgroup » as an independent combination (`docs/adr/0044-sandbox-tiers.md:69-97`), but the code refuses a cgroup without a PID namespace on purpose (`internal/sidecar/scope_linux.go:158-160`), as ADR 0042 requires with its `NoOrphan` proof (`docs/adr/0042-…:410-417`) | **accepted**: the cgroup bound exists only with a PID namespace; the namespace-free tier has the rlimit bound only | revision 2: the table loses that combination |
| C2 | critical | In the namespace-free tier descendants are not bounded: ordinary `clone` is allowed (`seccomp_linux.go:77-80`), only CORE, AS and FSIZE are set (`launcher_linux.go:183-194`), a group is killed after termination (`run.go:204,237`); a descendant can outlive the application, and a per-process AS bounds neither their number nor their total memory; `RLIMIT_NPROC` is per user, not per job | **accepted**, direction to validate by measurement (Q7): in that tier the sidecar may create threads but no process — `clone` only with the thread flags, `clone3` answered ENOSYS so that libc falls back to `clone`, `fork` and `vfork` denied, `execve` after start denied if it is not already — plus a parent-death signal on the sidecar; with no descendant the per-process `RLIMIT_AS` bounds the whole job and nothing outlives the application. | M1 | major | The probe is not the launch: the ADR caches a no-op launch with a bounded folder and `NoNetwork` (`0044:77-106`), while `run.go:368-369` and `mount_linux.go:153-155` allow other configurations; target, cwd, paths and policy can change after the probe | **accepted**: fixed common invariants and a named list of supported variants; each launch is checked before the document is exposed; a change after the probe is a test; an explicit refusal, never a silent downgrade | revision 2 |
| M2 | major | The two tests that never return on the runner are not diagnosed (`plan:45-46`). | M3 | major | The prlimit oracle is underspecified: the plan promises « pid 0 or self » (`plan:35`), but the filter gets a fixed pid before exec (`seccomp_linux.go:125`) and is inherited across fork, so that pid is not the descendant's | **accepted**: `prlimit64` is allowed with pid 0 only, no explicit pid; the tests cover the kernel's truncation of `pid_t`, negative and high values, the 64-bit pointer argument, CORE, a child, with and without a PID namespace, on amd64 and arm64 | revision 2, rule S-1 |

| M5 | major | The Landlock guarantees go beyond the frozen proofs: the ADR promises refusal of reading other `/proc` entries, of signals and of writes (`0044:61-64,120`), and the plan names no attack per guarantee and per tier (`plan:40-44`) | **accepted**: one escape attempt per guarantee, run inside each tier, with a verifiable sentinel: the parent's `environ` and `maps`, `/proc/self/root` links, writes and replacements outside the output folder, `tgkill` of an outside thread, IPC, and TCP, UDP and UNIX sockets. ### What the probe answers, and what it cannot

| Question | Fact | Settles |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | kernel, the AppArmor sysctls, the LSM list, the profiles that hold `userns` and the text of a shipped one | M7 (syntax), M9 (preconditions), whether the amd64 runner is restricted at all |
| Q2 | for an unconfined `unshare`: namespace created or which error, capabilities inside, `mount` of `/proc` and of a tmpfs | finding 3 of the cloud review; EPERM or EACCES |
| Q3 | the repository's own two probes, the raw error of each namespace set, `Run` plain and with `NoNetwork` (`TestSandboxProbeReport`, inert without `INKWELL_SANDBOX_PROBE=1`) | the disagreement of the probes, on a real machine |
| Q4 | Landlock ABI; a ruleset with TCP rules accepted or not | M6 |
| Q5 | systemd user session, the process's cgroup and its controllers, `systemd-run --user --scope` | why the scope is not delegated on the runner |
| Q6 | the two tests, with Go's goroutine dump if one does not return | M2 |
| Q7 | the system calls of the pinned typst and tinymist at start outside the sandbox; inside it, the calls answered ENOSYS, EPERM or EACCES and the signals | C2 (do they spawn?), M4, the `ppoll` hypothesis, m1 |
| Q8 | Q2 and Q3 again under a candidate profile, the labels of the process, of a child and of an extracted copy, and the same binary from an unprofiled path | M7, experiment E-2 |

It cannot answer: a desktop session (the runner has no graphical login, so its systemd user session may differ from a
person's), the installed `.deb` (the profile is tried on a test binary, not on the package), and anything about Fedora's
SELinux policy. Under `strace` the sandbox may behave differently (a tracer is attached): Q7's inside part is read
together with Q3's untraced result.
